Artificial turf testing, standards, and certifications

“Tested” is the beginning of a question. To understand a claim, identify the document type, exact sample, method and edition, laboratory, conditioning, result, pass criterion, intended use, and what was never evaluated.

4-minute readLast reviewed July 21, 2026Standards and claims guide

Document types are not interchangeable

Know what you are holding
DocumentPrimary purposeCommon misunderstanding
Test methodDefines how a property is measured under stated conditions.It may not set a pass/fail requirement.
SpecificationSets requirements or criteria for a stated scope.Compliance with one specification is not total system approval.
Guide or practiceDescribes procedures, considerations, or recommended practice.It may be voluntary and may not certify a product.
CertificationThird party attests that stated requirements and scheme rules are met.A mark can apply only to one product, system, facility, date, or use.
SDSCommunicates workplace chemical hazards and handling for a product/material.An SDS is not a complete consumer safety or environmental assessment.
Technical data sheetManufacturer’s declared construction, properties, and instructions.Values may be nominal and self-declared unless linked to reports.
Laboratory reportRecords a sample, method, conditions, results, and often uncertainty.A report for another SKU or component does not cover the proposed system.

Evidence strength

  1. Named exact product/component and current production identity.
  2. Independent sample selection or traceable chain of custody.
  3. Competent laboratory accredited for the stated method.
  4. Current method/standard edition and complete tested configuration.
  5. Result with units, detection limits or uncertainty where relevant.
  6. Applicable criterion and transparent pass/fail interpretation.
  7. Installed-system verification when performance depends on construction or maintenance.

ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation supports laboratory competence and impartiality, but only for methods within the laboratory’s accredited scope. A logo on a report is not enough; check the scope.

Sports certification versus residential suitability

FIFA evaluates football-specific player-surface interaction, ball behaviour, durability, safety, installation, and field performance. A FIFA certificate can be meaningful for the named football system; it does not establish residential aesthetics, pet hygiene, local drainage, municipal compliance, landscaping heat, or compatibility with a different base and infill. Conversely, a residential product without sports certification is not automatically inferior for a home lawn.

How to audit a report

  • Report number, issue date, page count, laboratory, accreditation, and accredited-scope evidence.
  • Client, manufacturer, exact product name, colour, component, sample source, batch, and receipt date.
  • Sampling plan and whether the laboratory, producer, seller, or installer selected the sample.
  • Method and edition, deviations, conditioning, ageing, substrate, infill, pad, and specimen orientation.
  • Results, units, replicate variation, uncertainty, detection/reporting limits, photographs, and raw observations where useful.
  • Requirement or comparison limit, jurisdiction/use, and who made the compliance decision.
  • Whether the report is complete, altered, expired, withdrawn, superseded, or tied to an earlier formulation.
