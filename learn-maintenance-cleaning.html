Artificial turf maintenance, cleaning, and repairs

Artificial turf removes mowing and regular watering, but it still needs a sensible routine. The right frequency depends on shade, trees, pets, traffic, infill, nearby construction, and the product instructions.

Cleaning pet areas

Remove solid waste, rinse as appropriate, and use only cleaners compatible with the exact turf backing, infill, adhesives, nearby plants, and drainage system. Strong chemicals can discolour fibres, damage backing, harm soil biology, or create a hazard for pets and people. Ask the installer for a written dilution and disposal routine. Odour that returns quickly can indicate a design, drainage, or cleaning problem rather than a need for more fragrance.

Leaves, pollen, weeds, and stains

Use a soft brush or blower that will not displace infill or damage fibres. Avoid aggressive power washing, metal rakes, solvents, open flame, and unapproved herbicides. Weeds can establish at edges, cracks, seams, and places where soil and seeds accumulate; keep borders clear and ask how repairs are handled. Blot spills quickly, follow the product care sheet, and test any cleaner in an inconspicuous area.

Snow, ice, and freeze-thaw

Snow itself is not a reason to treat the surface like a driveway. Ask the manufacturer about shovels, ploughs, salt, de-icers, snow blowers, and the direction of removal. Do not drag sharp metal across the turf or pile snow against doors, fences, foundations, or drainage outlets. Ice is a slip hazard; never assume turf is ice-free because it is synthetic. Spring inspection should include lifted edges, seams, compacted infill, and ponding.

Furniture, grills, fire, and heavy loads

Use broad, stable feet and move furniture rather than dragging it. Avoid concentrated loads, hot exhaust, embers, cigarettes, fire pits, and cooking equipment unless the complete installation and manufacturer specifically allow them. Heat can damage fibres and backing, while heavy loads can leave permanent impressions or affect drainage. Put protective pads under approved furniture and keep ignition sources on a suitable non-turf surface.

When a repair is needed

  1. Photograph the area and record when it was first noticed.
  2. Do not cut, glue, add infill, or apply chemicals before checking warranty and installer instructions.
  3. Identify whether the issue is surface wear, a seam, an edge, infill movement, subsidence, a drainage symptom, or damage from a third party.
  4. Ask for a written repair scope, colour-match limitations, cost, and warranty effect.
Maintenance is evidence: keep invoices, care products, weather observations, photos, and repair records. They help diagnose the real cause and support a warranty conversation.
