Four parts of the choice

What to Compare Together

These questions create a more useful conversation than a fixed savings claim or a universal promise.

How will the space be used?

Pets, children, putting practice, entertaining, heavy traffic, appearance, shade, and accessibility can point toward different surfaces and details.

What does the property require?

Access, excavation, soil, grading, drainage, edges, utilities, zoning, soft landscaping, trees, and adjacent surfaces affect feasibility and cost.

What care is realistic?

Artificial turf still needs debris removal, rinsing or cleaning where appropriate, brushing, inspection, heat awareness, and occasional repair or service.

What documents support the choice?

Review the exact product and infill specifications, itemized scope, assumptions, exclusions, price, care guide, and separate product and workmanship terms.

Before both of you agree

Put the Assumptions on Paper

A decision is easier when the proposal answers the practical questions and leaves less room for mismatched expectations.

  • Scope and priceWhat is included, excluded, assumed, optional, and subject to a documented change?
  • Schedule and disruptionWhat timing is expected, and what property access or homeowner decisions are required?
  • Appearance and samplesHave you reviewed the actual turf sample and relevant completed-project photos?
  • Ownership after installationWho handles routine care, damage, warranty notices, and eventual replacement?
