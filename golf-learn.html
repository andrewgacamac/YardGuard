00 · How to use this reference

Evidence first.
Site judgment always.

This guide separates what is established, what is regulated, what is manufacturer-specific and what must be designed for the property. That boundary matters.

A residential golf environment can borrow proven methods from golf-course architecture, agronomy, geotechnical design, stormwater practice and synthetic-turf construction. It should not copy them blindly. A professional green may support daily play and a full maintenance team; a private green may prioritize practice variety, winter resilience, quiet operation and integration with a home. The use case changes the correct answer.

AAuthority

Law, regulator or municipal requirement. Verify the current rule for the address.

TTechnical practice

Published guidance from USGA, ASGCA, government, university or an industry body.

MManufacturer

Product-specific information. Confirm the exact model and current installation manual.

PProject decision

A value that belongs in the design brief, drawings, specification or written proposal.

Transfer rule “Used on golf courses” is not the same as “appropriate for this residence.” Document the performance objective, site condition and maintenance capability first.

01 · Project definition

Write the game
before drawing the green.

The first deliverable should be a playing and ownership brief, not a shape. Record the primary users, dominant handedness, skill range, supervised or unsupervised use, desired shots, realistic maintenance appetite and seasons of operation. Separate “must perform” requirements from visual references.

Putting + social

A landscape that plays

  • Short and medium putts, broad circulation and forgiving contours
  • Several distinct cup approaches without crowding
  • Seating, lighting and views resolved with the playing surface
  • Usually synthetic where predictable care is a priority
Player development

A practice instrument

  • Repeatable start lines, measurable putt lengths and known breaks
  • Defined landing zones for chips and pitches
  • Surface targets expressed as ranges, not a single “fast” number
  • Data, video or projection coordinated from concept design

The ten-shot test

List the ten shots that justify the project: for example, a 1.5-metre pressure putt, a 6-metre pace putt, right-to-left and left-to-right breaks, uphill and downhill putts, a low runner, a greenside bunker shot, a 20-metre pitch and a full-swing simulator session. Then map the safe origin, landing area, rollout and miss zone of each shot. If the site cannot contain one safely, the brief—not physics—must change.

02 · Site investigation

Survey the whole system,
not only the lawn.

A defensible concept begins with a current legal/topographic survey and a field review. Locate property lines, easements, rights-of-way, buildings, trees, visible services, catch basins, downspouts, wells, septic components, retaining walls and existing drainage paths. Record spot elevations beyond the proposed green so the design understands where water arrives and where it can lawfully leave.

Investigate the growing environment even for synthetic turf. Shade, prevailing wind, leaf litter, seed drop, reflected heat, snow storage and irrigation overspray affect performance and care. For natural turf, solar exposure, air movement, irrigation capacity and water quality are fundamental agronomic inputs.

  1. 1

    Desktop due diligence

    Title survey, zoning, aerials, Conservation Authority mapping, municipal records and available servicing plans.

  2. 2

    Measured site inventory

    Topography, boundaries, structures, tree trunks and canopies, access widths, utilities and existing drainage.

  3. 3

    Subsurface investigation

    Test pits or geotechnical work appropriate to scope; identify soil profile, fill, groundwater observations and bearing concerns.

  4. 4

    Water investigation

    Catchment, roof discharge, low points, outlets, receiver constraints, irrigation source, pressure and water quality where relevant.

  5. 5

    Operational access

    Construction equipment and bulk material route, spoil handling, protection zones and long-term service access.

Hold point Do not freeze grading or drainage until the outlet, boundary conditions and below-grade conflicts are known.

Professional planning reference: ASGCA — Required Base Maps.

03 · Golf architecture

Design playable ground,
not decorative undulation.

A green complex includes the putting surface and its surrounds. Its success depends on approach options, cuppable areas, transitions, recovery choices, maintenance access, visibility and safe misses. ASGCA guidance emphasizes playability and the relationship between contours and the surrounding ground—not novelty for its own sake.

01

Useful contour

Each feature should create a readable putt, a strategic recovery or positive surface drainage.

02

Calm cup areas

Provide practical zones around cups; locate sharper transitions between, not through, intended hole areas.

03

Multiple routes

Reward a bold line while retaining a safer alternative for less-skilled players.

04

Maintainable edges

Avoid trapped slivers, inaccessible pockets and transitions that cannot be cleaned or repaired.

Speed is not design quality

The Stimpmeter was developed to compare putting-green speed consistently. Readings vary with technique, time, weather and location; the useful objective is a repeatable target range appropriate to contours and users. A surface that is too fast for its slopes reduces usable cup area and practice variety. Define acceptance by an agreed protocol and locations, not a sales claim.

Safety has no universal backyard setback

ASGCA’s practical-facility guidance says safety is the most important design consideration and also notes there is no single safety standard that replaces professional judgment. Model intended shots and credible misses toward people, glass, roads and adjoining property. Use orientation, distance, landform, planting, screens and use rules as a layered system. Netting is not permission to route an unsuitable shot.

References: ASGCA — Green Design; ASGCA — Building a Practical Golf Facility; USGA — Details Matter When Measuring Green Speed.

04 · Surface systems

Choose an assembly,
not a turf sample.

The visible surface is one component. Subgrade, separation, drainage, base materials, compaction, fine grading, edge restraint, cups, seams, infill and adjacent transitions determine whether a synthetic green performs. Natural turf adds drainage pipework, gravel and rootzone materials, irrigation, selected turfgrass and grow-in.

Decision framework: synthetic and natural putting surfaces

What “USGA green” actually means

The USGA construction method is a researched natural putting-green system with tested materials and defined layers, including subsurface drainage, gravel and a sand-based rootzone. The current recommendations address material selection, laboratory testing, perimeter drains, cleanouts and quality control. A sand-based green is not automatically a USGA green, and a synthetic green should never be described as one.

Synthetic specification questions

  • SystemIdentify every layer, its function and the party responsible for compatibility.
  • SubgradeRecord proofing, unsuitable-material response, separation and groundwater conditions.
  • BaseSpecify source, gradation or product, lift placement, compaction method and surface tolerance.
  • SurfaceRecord exact turf product, fibre/polymer construction, backing, infill and intended shot response.
  • DetailsDraw seams, cups, edges, drains, slopes, tie-ins and service penetrations.
  • EvidenceRequire current product data, relevant test reports, care manual and written warranty.

References: USGA — Beneath the Surface; USGA — Not All Sand-Based Greens Are USGA Greens; Synthetic Turf Council — Technical Guidelines. The STC is an industry body; use its guidance as a specification aid, not independent product endorsement.

05 · Water, drainage + winter

Design for the thaw,
not the photograph.

Southern Ontario is not one climate point. Environment and Climate Change Canada’s 1991–2020 normals show Toronto Pearson with sub-zero monthly mean temperatures in winter and warm summers; lake influence, elevation and local microclimate change conditions across the region. Use the nearest representative station and site observations, not a generic “Ontario” value.

Drainage has four questions: how water reaches the area, how it moves across the surface, how it moves through the assembly and where it discharges. A pipe without a suitable outlet is not a drainage design. The Ontario stormwater manual promotes integrated water-balance, quality, erosion and quantity objectives; municipal criteria and receiver conditions remain site-specific.

1Catchment

Rain, meltwater, roof leaders, irrigation and off-site inflow.

2Surface

Positive grades, no trapped pockets, protected thresholds.

3Profile

Permeability, separation, storage and internal conveyance.

4Outlet

Capacity, elevation, legality, erosion protection and access.

5Receiver

No adverse effect to buildings, neighbours or downstream systems.

Freeze–thaw design review

Check materials for stability when wet, positive surface drainage when the profile is frozen, edge restraint, transitions that could heave differently and components vulnerable to ice. Do not publish or accept a universal base depth: soil, excavation, drainage, loading, material properties and local frost conditions must inform the detail.

For natural greens, winter injury risk is closely connected to turf species, surface drainage, shade and ice/water behaviour. USGA case studies from Guelph and Toronto show severe consequences where poor surface drainage and annual bluegrass combine with Southern Ontario freeze–thaw conditions. Covers or heating measures are not generic cures; they change the microenvironment and need specialist planning.

References: ECCC — Toronto Pearson 1991–2020 Climate Normals; Ontario — Stormwater Management Planning and Design Manual; USGA — Cutten Fields Winter Injury; USGA — Islington Golf Club Green Rebuild.

06 · Ontario approvals + environment

Resolve jurisdiction
before design freeze.

There is no single “backyard golf permit.” The scope may involve grading, retaining walls, accessory structures, electrical work, plumbing, tree injury, fill movement, regulated land or water taking. Requirements differ by municipality and property. Obtain written direction where the answer affects design.

Approval screen for a Southern Ontario property

Locates are a two-part process

Ontario One Call instructs homeowners to request locates at least five business days before digging. Its public locate process does not identify all privately owned infrastructure. Gas to a barbecue or pool, landscape lighting, shed power, irrigation, septic components and security lines may require a private locator. Keep locate documentation current through the work.

Trees and soft landscaping can control the plan

Municipal rules vary. Toronto, for example, protects qualifying private trees and City trees and treats artificial turf as something other than “soft landscaping” for zoning calculations. This is an example, not a Southern Ontario-wide rule. Check the municipality before allocating the entire rear yard to hard surface or synthetic turf.

07 · Technology + life safety

Coordinate the room
around the swing.

Start with users and swing envelopes, then select tracking technology. Camera and radar systems have different mounting and ball-flight requirements. The structure, impact screen, enclosure, hitting position, projector, lighting, power, data, acoustics, ventilation, heating and service access must be coordinated as one room.

GeometryClear swing envelope

Test both handednesses, tallest user, club length, stance and circulation.

MeasurementModel-specific tracking

Follow the current manufacturer layout, mounting and calibration requirements.

BuildingStructure + services

Coordinate impact loads, fire/life safety, HVAC, electrical, data and acoustic control.

OperationSafe use protocol

Control access, player position, loose balls, screen condition and equipment startup.

Manufacturer dimensions are not interchangeable. TrackMan currently publishes different recommended room arrangements for its ceiling-mounted iO and behind-the-ball TrackMan 4 systems and advises consulting a representative because dimensions may vary. Treat published dimensions as an input to design—not a substitute for a measured swing test, code review or coordinated shop drawing.

Outdoor technology

Plan conduits, drainage at enclosures, Wi-Fi/data coverage, weather-rated equipment, glare control, service isolation and winter removal before hardscape is built. For projection or putting-analysis systems, confirm ambient-light limits, projector location, calibration surface, sightlines and licensing or subscription requirements in writing.

Manufacturer planning reference: TrackMan — Simulator space recommendations. Product claims and dimensions are manufacturer-supplied and must be verified for the selected model and installation.

08 · Construction + quality assurance

Inspect the work
before it disappears.

Premium construction is traceable. The drawings identify systems and interfaces; submittals identify the materials actually proposed; field records show what was installed; hold points prevent later layers from concealing unresolved work.

  1. 1

    Preconstruction review

    Issued drawings, approvals, locates, protection zones, access, erosion controls, material submittals and benchmark.

  2. 2

    Subgrade hold point

    Verify excavation limits, unsuitable conditions, elevations, proofing response and drainage path before separation/base layers.

  3. 3

    Drainage hold point

    Record pipe, structures, cleanouts, slopes, connections, outlet and testing before burial.

  4. 4

    Base hold point

    Verify material, lift placement, density or agreed field verification, moisture, levels and finish tolerance.

  5. 5

    Surface hold point

    Approve layout, grain direction if relevant, seams, cups, edges, transitions, infill and finished ball response.

  6. 6

    Commissioning

    Drainage demonstration, playability protocol, technology calibration, lighting controls, safety review, training and deficiencies.

The turnover record

  • As-builtFinished grading, drainage, utilities, conduits, sleeves and concealed components.
  • ProductsFinal approved submittals, batch/source records where appropriate and colour/product IDs.
  • TestingLaboratory and field results, calibration reports and acceptance observations.
  • CareSystem-specific operation, cleaning, winter and repair instructions.
  • WarrantyScope, exclusions, claim route, responsible parties and required owner maintenance.
  • BaselineCommissioning photos and agreed ball-roll/technology condition at handover.

Natural-green QA reference: USGA — Construction Recommendations Revision and Key Changes.

09 · Operations + performance

Manage condition,
not appearance alone.

A green should have an operating plan before handover. The plan identifies responsible people, frequencies, acceptable products, response to contamination or damage, seasonal shutdown and records. “Low maintenance” must never be interpreted as “no inspection.”

Minimum operating framework
IntervalSynthetic systemNatural turf system
RoutineRemove organic debris; inspect cups, edges, seams and high-traffic zonesMow with correctly maintained equipment; monitor moisture, clipping and turf stress
PeriodicBrush/groom as specified; redistribute or replenish approved infill; clean contaminantsTopdress, cultivate, roll, fertilize and manage pests from measured need
After stormsCheck ponding, washout, migration, edge movement and drainage structuresCheck ponding, erosion, disease risk, debris and irrigation/drainage function
SeasonalWinterize accessories; define safe snow/ice response; spring inspectionSpecialist winter preparation, monitoring, opening and recovery plan
AnnualProfessional condition review and repair forecastReview performance data, soil/rootzone testing and program outcomes

Measure consistently

For sophisticated natural greens, track surface speed, firmness, smoothness/trueness where equipment is available, volumetric water content, clipping yield, weather, inputs and maintenance events. Compare trends at consistent locations and times. The USGA recommends target ranges rather than a single number because conditions vary through the day and season.

Surface organic matter affects firmness, water movement and pest risk. The USGA’s current sampling method calls for consistent timing and representative intact samples; laboratory interpretation should guide topdressing and cultivation rather than a copied universal target.

References: USGA — Understanding Green Performance Numbers; USGA — Standard Method for Surface Organic Matter; OMAFRA — Integrated Pest Management for Turf; Guelph Turfgrass Institute.

10 · Owner’s specification

Ask for a project
that can be audited.

Use this schedule to structure a request for proposal, design brief or technical review. It is not a construction specification; it identifies the decisions and evidence a project-specific specification should contain.

01

Scope + parties

Define limits, exclusions, owner-furnished items, design responsibility, required professionals and interfaces.

02

Existing conditions

List survey, testing, utilities, trees, soil, water, approvals and information still to be verified.

03

Performance brief

Define users, shots, condition ranges, seasonal use, safety principles, aesthetics and maintenance model.

04

Drawings

Require layout, grading, drainage, sections, details, planting, lighting, technology and protection plans appropriate to scope.

05

Materials

Name exact proposed systems or performance requirements, evidence, compatibility, substitutions and samples/mockups.

06

Execution

State preparation, installation, tolerances, weather limits, protection, testing, hold points and correction process.

07

Acceptance

Set inspection sequence, measurable protocols, drainage check, play test, technology commissioning and deficiency closure.

08

Lifecycle

Require care manual, training, service access, spare materials, warranties, records and renewal/replacement planning.

Commercial reality A high price is not evidence of quality. Quality is the combination of design authorship, documented systems, qualified execution, inspection, commissioning and accountable aftercare.
