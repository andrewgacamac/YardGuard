How to think about artificial turf cost

A useful cost comparison shows what is included, what is assumed, and which numbers belong to your property. There is no honest universal price or guaranteed payback for every GTA yard.

3-minute readLast reviewed July 21, 2026Cost-planning guide

Start with the scope, not the headline number

Installed cost can change with area, access, excavation, soil, grading, drainage, edges, turf selection, infill, waste removal, labour, taxes, and optional work. Two quotes that look different may describe different projects. Two quotes with the same price may also hide different assumptions.

Best comparison question: “Can I see the included work, exclusions, site assumptions, selected products, options, taxes, and change process on one itemized proposal?”

Cost inputs worth recording

Inputs for a transparent project comparison
InputExamples of what to record
Property and sizeMeasured area, shape, slopes, access path, gates, stairs, and distance for material movement.
PreparationRemoval, excavation depth, soil condition, roots, disposal, aggregate, compaction, grading, and transitions.
System selectionTurf SKU, infill, seams, edging, drainage features, accessories, and product documentation.
Labour and scheduleExpected sequence, site access, homeowner decisions, disruption, cleanup, and weather dependencies.
Ongoing careRinsing, debris, pet-area routines, infill, furniture, snow handling, repairs, and optional services.
Comparison assumptionsNatural-grass service rates, water use, equipment, homeowner time, ownership period, replacement timing, taxes, and inflation.

Why payback claims need caution

A payback calculation is only as useful as its inputs. It can change with the actual project price, lawn-care frequency, water use, ownership period, care needs, replacement timing, and the value a homeowner places on time or use of the space. Presenting one number as a guarantee can make a complex personal decision look more certain than it is.

If you build a comparison, make it editable

  • Show the date, geography, area, tax treatment, and source of every input.
  • Let the homeowner change mowing, watering, service, care, and ownership assumptions.
  • Show upfront cost, ongoing cost, possible replacement, and excluded items separately.
  • Label the output as illustrative and invite a review of the real proposal.

What an itemized quote should help you decide

  1. Does the proposed system solve the actual use problem?
  2. Are the site conditions and exclusions understandable?
  3. Can you compare the proposal with another complete scope?
  4. Do the product and workmanship documents match the selected system?
  5. Do the expected care, disruption, and ownership responsibilities fit your household?
