Check the Rules Before You Build

Artificial turf is not automatically treated like natural grass. Zoning, required soft landscaping, drainage, grading, driveway limits, and property-specific overlays can all affect a project.

Municipality by municipality

What the Official Sources Say

These summaries are starting points, not blanket approvals. Confirm the current requirements for the exact property before construction.

Toronto

The City states that artificial turf is not soft landscaping and does not count toward required soft-landscaping area. Front- and rear-yard percentages vary with lot frontage and zoning.

Read Toronto's artificial-turf guidance and residential landscaping rules. Toronto directs owners to Toronto Building for property-specific confirmation.

Vaughan

Vaughan's Building Standards FAQ says artificial turf is not soft landscaping. Required soft or horticultural landscaping must therefore remain available where the zoning by-law requires it.

Read Vaughan's Building Standards FAQ. The City recommends contacting a Zoning Examiner for an address-specific review.

Brampton

Brampton's 2026 driveway standards amendment excludes artificial turf from the definition of residential permeable landscaping. Landscaping and driveway requirements still depend on the property and proposed work.

Read the City's notice for By-law 32-2026 (PDF) and confirm the current zoning interpretation with the City before changing a front yard or driveway-adjacent area.

Mississauga

Do not assume that artificial turf has blanket approval in every front or rear yard. The applicable zone, required landscaped area, driveway configuration, grading, drainage, and any site-specific provisions must be checked.

Use the City of Mississauga zoning information and ask the City to confirm how the current rules apply to the address and proposed scope.

Before the final proposal

Five Checks That Can Change the Scope

A site that looks straightforward can still have legal or physical constraints. We review applicable requirements with you, but the municipality or other authority makes the final determination.

  • Zoning and soft landscapingConfirm the required natural or horticultural area for the zone, lot frontage, and yard.
  • Grading and drainageCheck whether excavation, slope changes, stormwater flow, or a site-alteration permit affects the design.
  • Utilities and easementsLocate buried services and identify easements before excavation or anchoring.
  • Conservation or tree controlsDetermine whether conservation authority, ravine, tree-protection, or other overlays apply.
  • Shared-property rulesReview condominium, landlord, heritage, or private community requirements in addition to municipal rules.

Important: This page is general educational information, not legal advice or a municipal approval. Municipal rules and interpretations can change. Confirm the current requirements for your property directly with the municipality and obtain any required professional advice or approvals before work begins.
