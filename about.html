How We Approach a Project

Good project decisions come from accurate site information, defined responsibilities, and current product documentation.

Listen before recommending

We start with how the space needs to work, who will use it, what is failing today, and which tradeoffs matter to the homeowner.

Review the real conditions

Measurements, access, excavation, grading, drainage, utilities, edges, and municipal or private-property requirements can change the plan.

Put the scope in writing

The proposal should identify included work, known assumptions, exclusions, selected materials, price, expected timing, and how changes are approved.

Leave useful records

Product details, care instructions, applicable warranty documents, and completion information help the homeowner maintain the installation and raise future concerns.

Local Consultation, Site-Specific Scope

Current service availability depends on the address, project type, access, and schedule. Share your location and project details so we can confirm the next step.

  • ConsultationDiscuss the intended use, current problems, priorities, budget context, and approximate timing.
  • Property reviewConfirm the measurements and physical or regulatory conditions that affect the proposed work.
  • ProposalReview the system, scope, assumptions, exclusions, price, expected schedule, and applicable documents.
  • Installation and handoffComplete the accepted scope, conduct a final walkthrough, and provide relevant care and project information.

Contractor due diligence matters: Ask for current insurance, workplace coverage, trade membership, training, supplier, and product documentation relevant to your project. YardGuard will identify what is current and applicable during the proposal process rather than relying on an undated website badge.
