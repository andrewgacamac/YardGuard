General Questions

Basic information about artificial turf

Modern artificial turf is made from high-quality synthetic fibers, typically polyethylene or polypropylene. These materials are designed to look and feel like natural grass while being incredibly durable.

The turf is constructed in layers: the grass blades are attached to a backing material with drainage holes, and infill (usually silica sand or rubber) is added to keep the blades upright and add weight.

Appearance varies by product, pile height, blade shape, colour blend, infill, lighting, and installation.

Review physical samples and completed-project photos, and compare them in the conditions where the turf will be used.

Artificial turf can become hot in direct sun. Surface temperature varies with the product, infill, colour, shade, air temperature, and wind.

Check the surface before barefoot or pet use, provide shade, and follow heat-safety guidance. Water may cool the surface only temporarily.

Artificial turf can reduce routine irrigation, mowing, and lawn-treatment use, but it is a synthetic product with manufacturing, heat, runoff, maintenance, and end-of-life impacts. Whether it fits your goals depends on the site, intended use, care plan, and available alternatives.

Maintenance

Keeping your turf looking great

Artificial turf requires minimal maintenance compared to natural grass:

  • Monthly: Brush to keep blades upright (5 minutes)
  • As needed: Remove leaves and debris with a leaf blower
  • Occasionally: Rinse with water to remove dust
  • Annually: Inspect edges and seams

That's it! No mowing, watering, fertilizing, or pesticides needed.

Most spills can be cleaned with water and a mild soap solution:

  • Food/drinks: Rinse with water, use mild soap if needed
  • Grease/oil: Mild degreaser and water
  • Chewing gum: Freeze with ice, then scrape off
  • Paint: Mineral spirits or paint remover (test first)

For stubborn stains, we're always happy to provide guidance.

Drainage performance depends on the selected turf backing, infill, base design, soil, grading, available outlets, and rainfall conditions.

The site must be assessed and the proposed drainage approach documented. Artificial turf alone does not guarantee that a yard will not flood.

Municipal Rules & Bylaws

Know the rules for your city (Updated 2026)

Toronto states that artificial turf is not soft landscaping and does not count toward required soft-landscaping area.

Front- and rear-yard requirements vary by lot frontage, zoning, and the property. Review the City's artificial-turf guidance and residential landscaping rules.

Confirm the current requirements for the address with Toronto Building before work begins.

Mississauga: Do not assume blanket approval. The zone, required landscaped area, driveway, grading, drainage, and site-specific provisions can affect a project. Start with the City's zoning information and request address-specific confirmation.

Brampton: The City's 2026 driveway standards exclude artificial turf from the definition of residential permeable landscaping. Read the official By-law 32-2026 notice (PDF) and confirm the proposed scope with the City.

Vaughan: The City's Building Standards FAQ says artificial turf is not soft landscaping. Required natural or horticultural area must remain where the zoning by-law requires it. Review the official Vaughan FAQ and contact a Zoning Examiner for the address.

Richmond Hill: We do not rely on a general approval statement. Confirm the current zoning and property-specific requirements directly with the City.

There is no reliable one-size-fits-all answer. A project may involve zoning, soft-landscaping, grading, drainage, site-alteration, retaining-wall, driveway, conservation, utility, or private-property requirements.

This is general information, not legal advice or a municipal approval. Confirm the proposed work with the municipality and any other applicable authority.
