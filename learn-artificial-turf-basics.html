Artificial turf 101: what you are actually buying

Artificial turf is not one product. It is a surface system whose result depends on the turf, infill, base, drainage, edging, installation, and care working together.

3-minute readLast reviewed July 21, 2026Planning guide

The seven parts of a turf system

A useful comparison starts below the visible blades. Ask a contractor to identify each part of the proposed system and the assumption it relies on.

Questions to ask about each system component
ComponentWhat it doesWhat to document
TurfProvides the visible surface and affects appearance, feel, resilience, and care.Exact product/SKU, colour blend, pile, backing, installation limits, and current manufacturer documentation.
InfillHelps support blades and influences feel, weight, and maintenance.Material, amount, intended use, cleaning guidance, and any supported product claims.
BaseCreates a stable, shaped foundation below the surface.Excavation depth, aggregate, compaction, grading, soil conditions, and exclusions.
DrainageMoves water through or away from the complete site system.Existing outlets, slope, soil, low points, overflow path, and what happens during heavy rain.
EdgesHold the system in place and define its boundary.Edge material, transitions to paths or gardens, and how future movement is handled.
InstallationTurns the proposed materials into the finished surface.Scope, access, seams, cleanup, change process, schedule assumptions, and completion records.
CareMaintains appearance and helps owners respond to spills, debris, pets, and seasonal conditions.Written care guide, cleaning limits, snow/furniture guidance, and who handles future questions.
A practical rule: a turf sample can help you judge colour and feel, but it cannot tell you whether the proposed base, drainage, edges, or installation scope fits your property.

What “premium” should mean in a proposal

Words such as “premium,” “high performance,” or “pet friendly” are not a substitute for a product identity and project scope. Ask for the exact product name, current documentation, intended application, and the limitations that matter to your yard. If a claim depends on a test or declaration, ask what product or SKU the evidence covers.

What can change the outcome

  • Sun, shade, traffic, furniture, pets, and the way the yard will actually be used.
  • Existing soil, slopes, low points, downspouts, neighbouring drainage, and access for excavation.
  • Municipal, utility, easement, conservation, or private-property requirements that apply to the proposed scope.
  • Routine care, snow handling, repairs, and the written product and workmanship documents.

A better comparison checklist

  1. Compare complete itemized scopes, not just a turf roll or a price per square foot.
  2. Review a physical sample and relevant completed-project photos.
  3. Ask what is assumed, excluded, optional, or subject to a documented change.
  4. Keep the selected product information, care guide, invoice, completion photos, and applicable warranty documents.
