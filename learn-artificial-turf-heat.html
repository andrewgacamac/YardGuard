Artificial turf heat and summer safety in the GTA

Synthetic surfaces can heat up in direct sun. A successful design treats shade, water, timing, footwear, pets, children, and supervision as part of the system.

3-minute readLast reviewed July 21, 2026Health-aware planning guide

Why the surface can feel hot

Dark or dense synthetic materials absorb solar energy and may be hotter than nearby grass or soil. Air temperature, sun angle, wind, colour, pile, infill, shade, moisture, and surrounding hardscape all affect the result. A product label cannot predict the temperature of every installation. Ask for test conditions and do your own site-specific check on a hot day.

Plan shade first

  • Use existing tree shade where roots and canopies can be protected.
  • Consider retractable shade, pergolas, umbrellas, or a mixed hardscape zone designed for the load.
  • Place children’s and pet activity zones where shade is available during the hours they are used.
  • Do not install a tree or structure without considering utilities, drainage, roots, wind, footings, and future maintenance.

Use a simple surface check

Before allowing barefoot play, place a hand on the surface briefly and compare shaded and sunny areas. If it is uncomfortable, keep people and pets off it until it cools. A surface thermometer can provide a more repeatable record, but it is not a safety certification. Do not cool a hot surface with water if the runoff creates a slip hazard, drains toward a neighbour, or conflicts with the product care instructions.

Children and pets need separate guidance

For people using artificial turf, Toronto Public Health recommends heat precautions such as shade, drinking water, breaks, supervision, and avoiding very hot surfaces. That source is not veterinary guidance. For animals, the CDC advises fresh water in a shady area, while the Ontario SPCA advises limiting hot-weather activity, providing cool shelter, checking surfaces, and seeking prompt veterinary care when heatstroke or burned pads are suspected. This page is educational, not medical or veterinary advice.

Heat sources that should stay away

Do not place fire pits, grills, cigarettes, fireworks, hot exhaust, or embers on artificial turf. Use a separate non-combustible area designed for the appliance and keep sparks, grease, and heat away from fibres, backing, fences, plants, and structures. Ask the exact manufacturer about reflected heat from windows and nearby walls.

Source and limitations

Read the City of Toronto Public Health artificial-turf guidance, the CDC’s general pet-heat guidance, the Ontario SPCA hot-weather pet guidance, and the current product care sheet. Heat varies by site and weather; no universal “cool turf” promise is made here.
